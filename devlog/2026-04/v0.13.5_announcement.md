# BIF v0.13.5 Announcement — LinkedIn + Instagram

**Narrative:** Small-scope release, big emotional payoff. First time a skinned character walks in BIF. Pixar's HumanFemale walking is **the** hero shot — engine-agnostic, Qt-migration-proof, and instantly legible to any VFX person.

**Hero story:** CPU linear blend skinning + UsdSkel import end-to-end. The walk cycle plays. That's the whole pitch.

## LinkedIn post

BIF v0.13.5 — UsdSkel import shipped.

Quick follow-up on last week's v0.13.0. Smaller scope, but the payoff is something I've been wanting for a while: **skinned characters now walk in BIF.**

Pixar's HumanFemale.walk.usd loads end-to-end — 77 skinned prototypes, full rig, authored animation from frame 101 to 129 — and deforms correctly every frame when you scrub the timeline. Hair, buttons, shoes, all in the right place.

What landed:

→ **C++ bridge refactor** — `UsdSkelCache` + `UsdSkelSkeletonQuery` + `UsdSkelSkinningQuery` via `UsdSkelRoot::ComputeSkelBindings`, replacing raw attribute reads. Persistent skel cache on the stage so per-frame eval doesn't re-populate.
→ **New FFI** — `usd_bridge_compute_skel_skin_xforms(stage, skel_idx, time_code, out, capacity)` — evaluates SkeletonQuery at a given time code. Rust wrapper: `UsdStage::compute_skel_xforms(skel_idx, t)`.
→ **`bif_core::skinning` module** — `compute_skin_matrices` builds the palette (joint_skel × inv_bind × geom_bind). `skin_positions` weighted blend per vertex. `skin_normals` uses inverse-transpose so non-uniform-scale joints produce correct shading normals. Out-of-range joint indices are skipped, not panicked.
→ **Multi-draw skinning path** — scenes with multiple skinned prototypes each get LBS into per-prototype GPU vertex buffers. Mirrors the existing vertex-animation hot path.
→ **Six follow-up fixes** during HumanFemale validation: per-mesh joint-order remap, UV-seam vertex expansion, rigidly-deformed mesh broadcast, SkelRoot world xform override, skipping per-frame xform anim for skinned meshes, and a latent SSO-related UAF in `joint_path_ptrs` fixup.
→ **14 new tests** — cpp_bridge, loader, and skinning unit tests. Animated character eval asserts joint motion over the authored 101–129 range (max delta 39.5).

Things that still need work: GPU skinning (currently CPU only), blend shapes, skinning a mesh with multiple materials cleanly. v0.13.5 is the milestone where "characters deform at all" — v0.14+ is where it gets fast and complete.

This is also the kind of feature where the proof is the video, not the screenshot. Watch the HumanFemale walk and you either see it working or you don't.

## Capture shot list — v0.13.5

**Rule:** The walk cycle is the entire pitch. Everything else is supporting material. Lead with the walk.

### Video shots (Reels / LinkedIn video — 15–30 seconds, video-first)

1. **The walk** (5–8s) — Pixar HumanFemale full turntable loop as the walk cycle plays. Orbit the camera slowly. This is shot #1, shot #2, and shot #3. Do not get cute with cuts here. Let it walk.
2. **Timeline scrub** (2–3s) — on-screen capture of you dragging the timeline, watching the character deform in real time. Shows it's live eval, not a pre-baked loop.
3. **Close-up: a joint in motion** (2–3s) — tight on the elbow or knee as the character moves. Shows the deformation is correct, not sliding-skin failure.
4. **Bind pose vs animated frame** (3s) — side-by-side or quick swipe: T-pose on left, walking frame on right. Classic rigging demo shot.
5. **Two-bone arm test** (2s) — clean pedagogical shot of the `two_bone_arm.usda` test asset bending. Says "this is verified, not just demo magic."
6. **Terminal: tests passing** (2s) — `cargo test -p bif_core skinning` showing 14 new tests green.
7. **End card** — "BIF v0.13.5 · UsdSkel import · github.com/byvfx/bif"

### Photo shots (Instagram — video is better, but if you need stills)

1. **Frame 1 — Hero walk frame.** Full character mid-stride, hero lighting (HDRI dome light from v0.13.0 — payoff across releases). No UI.

### B-roll (nice-to-haves)

- Wireframe overlay of the skin on the character (if your viewport has wireframe mode — you do, per M31)
- Joint visualization / skeleton overlay (if debug drawing exists)
- Animated progression: bind pose → mid-pose → full pose → return (stop-motion feel)
- Slow zoom on the hair and buttons (the "everything stays in the right place" sell)

### Capture tips

- Record the walk cycle at 60fps minimum, at higher resolution than the final export — you want to survive compression
- Lighting: use a good HDRI for the hero walk. Mood > color. The character is the subject.
- Background: neutral dark or a simple gradient. No busy scenes behind the character.
- The walk has ~29 frames of authored anim (101–129) — loop it seamlessly by cutting on the same contact frame
- Keep camera moves subtle and slow. Let the deformation do the work, not the cinematography
- If the viewport is egui-themed, crop tight enough that chrome doesn't dominate — or better, record the render output fullscreen via an offline render pass
